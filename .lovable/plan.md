# Three warm Revoo outreach emails for Icelandic clients

## Goal
Create three polished English emails for 52 former Icelandic visualization clients. The emails will introduce Marius’s expanded work through Revoo without sounding like mass cold outreach.

## Versions
1. **Relationship-led** — reconnect through the work already done together, then introduce Revoo and invite a reply for demo access.
2. **Business-value-led** — clearly present the two property-sales and rental-management directions, emphasizing less manual work and stronger control.
3. **Referral-led** — written for recipients who may not operate rentals themselves, with a respectful, easy request to forward the message or make an introduction.

Each version will include:
- 3–5 subject-line options;
- a concise email body suitable for personal sending;
- optional personalized opening text;
- one simple call to action;
- a natural signature and discreet opt-out line.

## Message structure
- Lead with the existing relationship and sincere appreciation.
- Explain that the team now does more than visualization:
  - sales websites/platforms for brokers, agencies, and developers;
  - Revoo operational platforms for rental businesses.
- Present Revoo’s rental directions:
  - long-term portfolios: units, tenants, leases, meter readings, issues, invoices, balances, and vacancy inquiries;
  - hotels and short-term/vacation rentals: direct booking website, reservations, pricing, guest emails, invoicing, housekeeping, maintenance reporting, analytics, and integrations.
- Link to `revoo.site` for context and offer private access to a working hotel demo.
- Ask for either a demo conversation or an introduction to a relevant Icelandic operator.

## Dale Carnegie principles
- Begin with genuine appreciation rather than a sales claim.
- Speak from the recipient’s interests: time saved, fewer scattered tools, direct bookings, clearer operations, and better guest service.
- Avoid criticism of their current setup and avoid pressure.
- Make the recipient feel helpful and respected when asking for an introduction.
- Use a small, easy next step: reply with “demo” or forward the email.
- Keep the tone personal, conversational, and specific.

## Evidence and claim boundaries
- Describe the short-term/hotel platform as working and demo-ready.
- Describe long-term rental management as a dedicated new solution already under development, not as a fully launched public product.
- Do not promise live card processing or unverified integrations; refer more generally to payments and integrations where appropriate.
- Avoid exaggerated claims such as “all-in-one for every business” or guaranteed savings.

## Final recommendation
After the three drafts, recommend the strongest version for this warm Icelandic audience and briefly explain which recipient type fits each alternative.
